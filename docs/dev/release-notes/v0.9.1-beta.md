**PFWolf** (Project-Friendly Wolfenstein 3D) is a source port of Wolfenstein 3-D, Spear of Destiny
and Blake Stone, built so you can make a game, or change one, with data files instead of
changing the engine. It comes with **PFWolf Editor**, a map and content editor for the same files.

This is PFWolf's first public release. It's a **beta**: everything below is in and working, but
it hasn't been played on many machines yet. Expect rough edges, and please report them.

## Downloads

| System | Download |

| Windows (64-bit) | `pfwolf-0.9.1-win-x64.zip` |
| Linux x64 | `pfwolf-0.9.1-linux-x64.tar.gz`, or `PFWolf-0.9.1-x86_64.AppImage` |

Each has the game, the editor and 44 example mods. Nothing else needs installing: .NET, SDL2
and OpenAL Soft come with it.

## What you need

PFWolf plays the original games' data files, which aren't included. It runs:

- **Wolfenstein 3-D**: the retail release (as sold on Steam and GOG), the Apogee release and the
  free shareware episode
- **Spear of Destiny**, and the Spear of Destiny demo
- **Blake Stone: Aliens of Gold** (and its shareware episode) and **Blake Stone: Planet Strike**
- **Standalone games** made with PFWolf, which need no other game's files

Copy the data files (`VSWAP.WL6`, `GAMEMAPS.WL6` and the rest) beside the game on Windows and
Linux, or into your PFWolf data folder: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\PFWolf` on Windows,
`~/.local/share/PFWolf` on Linux, `~/Library/Application Support/PFWolf` on a Mac (where it's the
only place). Upper or lower case file names both work.

## The first run

- **Windows:** unzip anywhere and run `PFWolf.exe` (the editor is `PFWolf.Editor.exe`). The
  programs aren't signed yet, so SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC": choose
  **More info → Run anyway**.
- **Linux:** untar anywhere and run `./PFWolf`; `./install-desktop-entries.sh` adds the game and
  the editor to your application menu. Or make the AppImage executable and run it: it's the game,
  and `--editor` opens the editor.
- **macOS:** unzip, then drag **PFWolf.app** and **PFWolf Editor.app** into Applications. They
  aren't notarized yet, so the first time macOS won't open them: right-click the app and choose
  **Open** (on macOS 15 and later: try once, then **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open
  Anyway**). In the editor, choose PFWolf.app as the game folder.

## What's in it

### Playing

- **The originals as they were:** Wolfenstein 3-D's and Spear's own demos play back
  frame-for-frame as in the original game, and Blake Stone and Planet Strike follow bstone's
  behaviour closely.
- **Modern comforts:** mouse look, widescreen with a UI scale, an uncapped frame rate,
  rebindable keys, mouse wheel and controller, timestamped saves with thumbnails, quick saves
  and autosaves, optional on-screen messages.
- **Multiplayer for up to 4 players** over LAN or online: co-op (with joining mid-game),
  deathmatch, team deathmatch, capture the flag, heist, domination, king of the hill, horde and
  prop hunt. The Multiplayer mod adds deathmatch arenas, extra weapons and playing as enemies.

### Making your own

- **Mods** are folders, `.zip` or `.pk3` files: load them with `--file`, by dropping them on the
  game, or from **Options → Mods**. A mod gives only what it changes; its YAML is laid over the
  game's key by key.
- **A renderer to build with:** textured floors and ceilings, skies, walls of any height with
  arches and stories you can walk on top of, lifts and ramps, 45° diagonal walls, wall patches,
  see-through doors and fences, destructible walls, animated and scrolling walls and flats,
  distance fog, night levels, light zones, coloured lights and muzzle flashes, particles, rain
  and snow.
- **Everything is data:** actors, weapons, player classes, factions and followers, vehicles,
  doors and keys, wall switches, hub levels, fonts, texts, palettes, title screens and screen
  transitions.
- **Lua scripting** for new actions, event hooks, HUD drawing and puzzle mini-games, sandboxed
  so that demos, saves and network games stay in step.
- **Your own art and sound:** PNG pictures, WAV/OGG/MP3 sounds, IMF/OGG/MP3 music; levels as
  ECWolf `.wad`, UWMF or GAMEMAPS, up to 256×256.
- **Standalone games** that need no original data files, and **classic mods**: point
  `--make-mod` at a folder of WDC- or ChaosEdit-edited data files to play it as a PFWolf mod.

### PFWolf Editor

- **Levels:** every map plane, brushes and stamps, shaped selections, replace, rotate, flip and
  resize, problem checks, and **Play** (F5) to try the level in the game straight away.
- **A 3D view** beside the map, lit as the game lights it, where you can pick and edit.
- **Browsers and editors** for textures, flats, sprites and pictures (with a pixel editor), actors
  and their states, fonts and glyphs, sounds and music (with an IMF song editor, MIDI import and
  AdLib and PC-speaker sound editors), palettes, texts, Lua scripts and recorded demos.
- **Definitions** (F11): field editors for game-info, game packs, actors, mapdefs, factions and
  animations, or the raw YAML.
- **New mod…** starts a mod, or a standalone game that plays as it's made.

### Example mods

The `mods` folder comes with 44 example mods to switch on in **Options → Mods**, each showing one
feature: rooftops and lifts, vehicles, puzzles, particles, weather, lighting, skies, hub levels,
destructible walls, custom fonts, a standalone game and more. The
[examples README](https://github.com/littleagames/pf-wolf/blob/main/examples/README.md) says
what each one shows.

## What "beta" means here

From 0.9 to 1.0, PFWolf gets fixes, speed-ups and documentation, not new features. The things
mods are made of (`modinfo.yaml`, the YAML definitions, the Lua API, UWMF levels and save games)
stay compatible; when one has to change, the release notes say how.

- **Saves from the alpha builds don't load** in 0.9.1: the save format has changed since then.
- **In a multiplayer game everyone needs the same version.**

## Known issues

<!-- decide: add or remove anything you know of from testing -->
- **The Linux and macOS builds are new.** They build and pass the automated tests, but few people
  have played them yet.
- **The programs aren't signed:** SmartScreen asks on Windows, and Gatekeeper on a Mac (see
  [The first run](#the-first-run)).
- **The editor on a Mac uses the Control key**, not Command, for its shortcuts.
- **With the uncapped frame rate,** the view and actors move smoothly, but sliding doors,
  pushwalls and the weapon bob still move in the game's 70-a-second steps.
- **Blake Stone and Planet Strike** are close to the originals, but a few behaviours still
  differ.
